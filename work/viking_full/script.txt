[0:00 – HOOK]

You’re the only woman on an island of 100 Vikings. And every single one of them just dropped to his knees.

Twelve hours ago, your sailboat broke apart in a North Atlantic storm. You held on to a piece of the mast until the waves threw you onto a black stone beach. When you opened your eyes, a hundred warriors were standing over you, and then they knelt.

JARL: One moon. Thirty days.

That’s the law here. Anything the sea gives back stays for thirty days. On the thirtieth night, all hundred warriors vote with their axes. If every axe goes up, you stay forever. If even one stays down, you go back to the sea.

But here’s what nobody tells you on day one: someone on this island wants you dead before the vote ever happens.

By day 30, you’ll know who. And you won’t believe why.

[0:50 – DAY 1: THE DAUGHTER OF THE SEA]

Day one. Nobody ties your hands. Nobody points a weapon at you. They wrap you in furs and carry you up to the village like you’re made of glass.

You don’t understand a word they say. Then you catch one: “Rán.”

Your grandmother was Norwegian, and Norwegian grew out of Old Norse, the language the Vikings spoke. Icelandic stayed so close to it that Icelanders today can still read sagas written 800 years ago. So you understand one word in three. It’s enough to work out that they think you’re the daughter of Rán.

In Norse myth, Rán was the goddess of the sea. She caught drowning sailors in her net and took them to her hall under the waves. Her nine daughters were the waves.

You’re not going to correct them.

Then a young man pushes through the crowd. He’s tall, with a cold smile. This is Hakon, the Jarl’s son.

HAKON: If she is Rán’s daughter… then the sea cannot drown her.

Nobody says a word.

[2:00 – DAY 2: THE TEST]

Day two. They row you out past the rocks at dawn, the whole village watching from the cliffs.

HAKON: Go home to your mother.

And they push you in.

The water is so cold your chest locks. You can’t breathe. Your boots fill up and pull you down, and the light above you gets smaller and smaller…

Then a hand grabs your jacket and pulls you up hard. You hit the surface. A wave throws you onto the rocks, and when you look back, there’s nobody there.

But there’s something in your fist that wasn’t there before: a wolf’s tooth on a leather cord.

On the cliff, a hundred Vikings are cheering. The sea gave you back, so you really must be Rán’s daughter. Only Hakon isn’t cheering. He’s staring at the water, trying to work out who saved you.

So are you. Keep that wolf tooth in mind.

[3:05 – DAY 4: THE EMPTY CHAIR]

Day four. The Jarl gives you a job.

JARL: Rán’s daughter knows the sea. You will read it for us.

You’re their new navigator. You ask what happened to the old one.

JARL: Gunnar. He walked into the fog one night and did not come back. The sea took him.

You get Gunnar’s hut. Under his bed you find a wooden chest. Inside are his tools and a strip of bone carved with runes you can’t read.

Then you look closer at the blanket. It’s covered in dark stains, the kind fog doesn’t leave.

Gunnar didn’t walk into the sea. Somebody killed him the night before you arrived.

And women at the helm isn’t just a story. In 1878, archaeologists in Sweden opened a famous Viking warrior’s grave at Birka. It held a sword, an axe, a spear, arrows, two horses, and a strategy board game, so it looked like the grave of a commander. For 130 years everyone assumed it was a man. In 2017, DNA proved the warrior was a woman.

[4:10 – DAY 6: THE FEAST]

Day six. The village throws a feast in your honor: smoked fish, mutton, mead, and drums. Hakon himself brings you a plate.

HAKON: For the daughter of the sea.

But before you can eat, a big warrior called Torstein grabs a piece off your plate and laughs.

TORSTEIN: Goddesses don’t get hungry!

Thirty seconds later, he’s shaking. Then he’s on the floor, and then he stops breathing.

Back home, you’re a lifeguard, so you do the one thing nobody here has ever seen. You tilt his head back and push on his chest, thirty times, then two breaths, again and again. The whole hall goes silent.

Modern CPR was only invented around 1960. To these people, you look like you’re pushing life back into a dead man.

Then Torstein coughs. He’s alive. And that food was meant for you.

[5:15 – DAY 8: THE TRIAL]

Day eight. You’d think saving a man would make you a hero. Instead, Hakon stands up at the assembly.

HAKON: The food was on HER plate. She brought death to our feast. She is no goddess. She is a witch.

Now you’re on trial for poisoning the man you saved.

But you saw Torstein’s symptoms: shaking, foaming at the mouth, seizures. That’s water hemlock, one of the most poisonous plants in Europe, and it only grows in wet ground.

YOU: Where is the marsh on this island?
JARL: …The north side.
YOU: I’ve never been to the north side. You’ve watched me every day. Who has?

The hall goes quiet. Nobody answers, but somebody’s boots are still caked in black marsh mud. You can’t prove anything yet, so you say nothing.

You’re cleared. But now the killer knows you’re hunting him.

[6:10 – DAY 11: THE STORM]

Day eleven. The sky is clear and blue, and the men are loading the longships for a big fishing trip.

Then you glance at your diving watch. It has a barometer, and the pressure is dropping fast. When air pressure crashes like that, a storm is coming.

YOU: Nobody goes out. A storm is coming.
HAKON: There isn’t a cloud in the sky!

The Jarl looks at you for a long moment, then he orders the ships to stay on the beach.

Three hours later, the storm hits so hard it tears roofs off the longhouses. If those ships had gone out, half the village would be at the bottom of the sea.

Forty axes go up that night, just for you.

But the next morning, walking the beach after the storm, you see a thin line of smoke rising from the forest on the far side of the island. The Jarl told you there are only a hundred people here. So who lit that fire?

[7:05 – DAY 14: THE OUTLAWS]

Day fourteen. You follow the smoke deep into the forest and walk straight into a knife.

There are five men living in the trees. They’re thin and wild, dressed in wolf skins. They’re outlaws.

That’s real Viking law. The worst punishment wasn’t death, it was being made an outlaw, a skógarmaðr, a “forest man.” Nobody could feed you, shelter you, or help you, and anyone could kill you. The famous outlaw Grettir the Strong survived like that for almost twenty years.

Their leader steps forward. He has grey in his beard and the same eyes as the Jarl.

ULF: I am Ulf. The Jarl is my brother.

Ten years ago, the village grain store burned down in the middle of winter. People starved, and Ulf was blamed. His own brother made him an outlaw.

[7:55 – DAY 17: THE RUNES]

Day seventeen. You keep sneaking back to the outlaw camp with food. Ulf teaches you to set snares, and you teach him how to make a fishing net that doesn’t tangle.

Then you show him Gunnar’s runes, and Ulf goes pale.

ULF: It says… Ulf did not light the fire. I saw who did. I will speak at the Thing.

Gunnar knew the truth for ten years. He finally decided to tell everyone, and the very next night he died.

[8:40 – DAY 20: THE WOLF TOOTH]

Day twenty. You’re sitting by Ulf’s fire when you notice the cord around his neck. It’s the same leather, but it’s empty.

You pull the wolf tooth out of your pocket.

ULF: (quietly) I watch the village from the cliffs every day. I saw them throw you in.
YOU: Why did you save me?
ULF: Because I know what it’s like when your whole village decides you belong to the sea.

[9:15 – DAY 23: THE KILLER]

Day twenty-three. The pieces finally fit together.

Ten years ago, Hakon was sixteen. He was playing with fire in the grain store and burned it down. He let his uncle take the blame, and his father the Jarl believed him.

Gunnar saw it, and Gunnar was about to tell everyone, so Hakon killed him. Then you washed up, and the Jarl gave you Gunnar’s hut and Gunnar’s chest with the runes. That’s why Hakon wanted you dead from day one.

You go to the north marsh and find fresh holes where water hemlock roots were dug up, and boot prints the size of Hakon’s.

[9:50 – DAY 26: THE THING]

Day twenty-six. You call a Thing, the Viking assembly where disputes were settled in front of everyone. It’s real history: Iceland’s Thing, the Althing, began in the year 930 and is one of the oldest parliaments in the world. A lawspeaker stood on a great rock and recited the law from memory.

You stand on that rock and tell them everything: the fire, Gunnar, the poison, the runes.

Hakon just smiles.

HAKON: The word of an outlaw means nothing under our law. And the word of a foreign woman means even less.

Then forty warriors step behind him. They’re his friends, his crew, men he’s been buying with silver for years.

And you realize what that means. Forty axes will never go up for you. You’ve already lost the vote.

[10:45 – DAY 28: THE FATHER]

Day twenty-eight. The Jarl comes to your hut, alone, in the middle of the night.

JARL: I knew.

He knew Hakon set the fire, from the very first night.

JARL: He was my only son. So I sent my brother to the forest instead. I have carried it for ten years.
YOU: And Gunnar?
JARL: (long pause) That… I did not know.

He looks a hundred years old.

JARL: In two nights, you will lose the vote. I cannot save you. I could not even save my brother.

[11:20 – DAY 30: THE VOTE]

Day thirty, the last night. Torches burn on the cliff, and a hundred Vikings stand in a circle with their axes.

JARL: Daughter of the sea. Ask the Thing to let you stay.

You don’t. Instead, you say:

YOU: I ask the Thing to bring Ulf home.

Silence. Hakon laughs.

HAKON: Forty axes say no.

Then Torstein steps out of Hakon’s forty, the man who died at the feast and came back.

TORSTEIN: I was dead on that floor, and your plate put me there, Hakon. She brought me back.

He raises his axe. Then another man steps out, and another, and one by one Hakon’s forty fall apart.

Finally, only two axes are still down: Hakon’s, and his father’s.

The Jarl looks at his son for a very long time. Then he raises his axe.

JARL: Brother. Come home.

Ulf walks out of the trees, and the whole cliff roars.

Hakon gets the sentence he gave his uncle: the forest.

[12:35 – THE FINAL TWIST]

Then the Jarl turns to you.

JARL: Now. The vote for you.

But nobody raises an axe. Instead, all hundred warriors lay their axes on the ground at your feet.

ULF: By our law, the one who brings justice to the Thing doesn’t stay as a guest. She becomes the Lawspeaker.

You washed up as a stranger, and thirty days later you’re the voice of their law.

The next morning, on day thirty-one, a fishing boat appears on the horizon. It’s the first ship in ten years, and it could take you home.
